<M2>GOAL</M2>Simplify a fraction so that its denominator has no <Tn>**radicals**<Fragment slot="tip">The $\sqrt{\phantom{H}}$ symbol</Fragment></Tn>.

### Steps

<PNT>

If your denominator looks like <H>$a\sqrt {c\vphantom{k}}$</H> for $a,c\in\mathbb Q$, multiply the numerator and denominator by $\V{\sqrt{c\vphantom{k}}}$.<Note>By the way, don't forget to simplify any radicals first before you begin, like turning $\sqrt8$ into $2\sqrt2$. This usually makes the calculations easier!</Note>

</PNT>

<DRP2>
<M2>SEE IT</M2>Simplify $\dfrac{6}{5\sqrt3}$.
<Fragment slot="drop">

Solution: Multiplying top and bottom by $\Vx{\sqrt{3\vphantom k}}$, we have $\dfrac{6}{5\sqrt3} = \dfrac{6\cdot\Vx{\sqrt3}}{5\sqrt3 \cdot \Vx{\sqrt3}} = \dfrac{6\sqrt3}{5\cdot3}$ since $\sqrt{3\vphantom k}\cdot\sqrt{3\vphantom k}=3$. Notice that we can now cancel out a $3$ in the numerator and denominator, and we are left with $\boxed{\dfrac{2\sqrt3}{5}}$, which is fully simplified.
</Fragment>
</DRP2>

<Spa height="3"/>

<PNT>

If your denominator looks like <H>$a+b\sqrt{c\vphantom{k}}$</H> when simplified (for $a,b,c\in\mathbb Q$), multiply the numerator and the denominator by the denominator's <V>**conjugate**</V>, $\V{a-b\sqrt{c\vphantom{k}}}$. <Tn><S>_Why does this work?_</S><Fragment slot="tip">This works because of the <Vx>**difference of squares factoring**</Vx>, which gives us $\Vx{(a+b\sqrt{c\vphantom{k}})(a-b\sqrt{c\vphantom{k}}) = a^2-b^2c}\ \in\ \mathbb Q$.</Fragment></Tn>

</PNT>

<PNT_A>

Note that this also applies for denominators like $a\sqrt b+c\sqrt d$, but more complex ones will require more work. (See examples & exercises.)

</PNT_A>

<DRP2>
<M2>SEE IT</M2>Simplify $\dfrac{2}{3+4\sqrt2}$.
<Fragment slot="drop">

Multiplying top and bottom by $\Vx{3-4\sqrt2}$, we have $\dfrac{2}{3+4\sqrt2}=\dfrac{2\cdot\Vx{(3-4\sqrt2)}}{(3+4\sqrt2)\cdot\Vx{(3-4\sqrt2)}}$.

The numerator equals $6-8\sqrt2$ after distributing the $2$. The denominator, by difference of squares, equals $3^2-(4\sqrt2)^2=9-16\cdot2=-23$.

So all in all, we have $\dfrac{6-8\sqrt2}{-23}=\boxed{\dfrac{8\sqrt2}{23}}$.
</Fragment>
</DRP2>
